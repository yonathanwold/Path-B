# Path B - Codex Context

## Hackathon

Path B is a student project for the Stellic Pathfinders Challenge. The challenge asks students to create something that helps people navigate their college journey and what comes after.

Our team has three members. Path B is primarily entered in **Overcoming Obstacles**, with **Degree Planning & Discovery** as its secondary category.

The project should be useful, believable, and easy to understand. The submission will include a project title and category, a short write-up, a two-minute demo video, a working link, and a list of tools used.

## Project identity

**Name:** Path B  
**Subtitle:** A crash test for your college plan.

**Simple description:** Path B helps students understand how their academic plan would hold up if real life changed, then shows realistic ways forward based on what they need to protect.

## The student problem

College plans often look clear until something unexpected happens. A student might fail an important course, need to work more hours, take care of family, face a financial-aid problem, lose access to summer classes, or be blocked by a registration or paperwork issue.

These changes can affect future courses, graduation timing, cost, workload, and eligibility. Students often discover the consequences only after the disruption has happened. They may feel stuck without knowing what is affected, what choices remain, or who can help.

## The core idea

Path B lets a student test realistic disruptions against their current college plan. It helps them see:

- which parts of the plan are most vulnerable;
- which future courses or requirements could be affected;
- whether graduating on time is still possible;
- what the change could mean for cost, workload, and financial aid;
- what different paths are available; and
- what questions to bring to an advisor or another support office.

Students can choose what they are trying to protect, such as graduating on time, keeping a job, staying eligible for financial aid, avoiding summer classes, avoiding an overloaded semester, or staying near home. Different priorities can lead to different paths. Path B should make those tradeoffs visible instead of pretending there is one perfect answer.

## Example student story

Maya is a sophomore computer science student who works part-time and needs to remain enrolled at least half-time. She fails a required course that is offered only once a year and is a prerequisite for several later courses.

Path B helps Maya understand why that course was a vulnerable point, what parts of her future plan are affected, and what choices she has. It can show a path that protects graduating on time, a path that protects her work schedule, and a path that reduces her course load but may delay graduation. It also gives her practical questions to discuss with an advisor.

## What makes Path B different

The central idea is **degree-plan resilience**: helping a student understand whether their path can survive real life.

Path B focuses on the combination of:

- stress-testing a plan before or immediately after a disruption;
- showing hidden weak points and dependencies;
- treating work, finances, health, family responsibilities, and personal limits as real parts of planning;
- letting students choose what they want to protect;
- showing several paths and their tradeoffs; and
- ending with a practical human next step.

Path B should not become a generic degree planner, a prettier degree audit, or a chatbot that gives vague academic advice. It should not tell students what choice to make. Its purpose is to make their choices and consequences easier to see.

## Project promise

> Before a disruption becomes a derailment, Path B helps students see what is still possible and choose a path forward.

When working on this project, keep the experience clear, honest, supportive, and grounded in the reality of student life. The goal is to replace panic and uncertainty with a better understanding of options and a more useful conversation with the right human support.
